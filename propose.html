Proposal guide

Propose an IACR Cryptology School.

The published IACR policy sets out the proposal format, funding model and review process. This redesign turns those requirements into a practical checklist.

Proposal format

Seven things your proposal should cover

Name and topic

Give a concise description and explain why the topic is relevant and timely for cryptology research.

Objective

Describe the goals, expected participant benefits, prerequisites, skills or knowledge to be imparted, and materials to be made available.

Estimated attendance

Give a realistic estimate of the expected number of participants.

Location, time and accessibility

Cover travel, local transportation, housing, venue arrangements, accessibility and relevant seasonal constraints.

Potential speakers

List speakers and indicate which are committed or soft-committed.

Talk format

Describe the teaching format, schedule, session lengths and structure.

Budget

Give a detailed budget, other funding sources, deficit responsibility and a no-profit plan. IACR support is partial or seed funding and does not transfer financial responsibility to IACR.

Evaluation

What reviewers look for

Research relevance

A timely topic with a clear connection to cryptology and future research.

Educational impact

Strong potential to benefit participants and stimulate research.

People

High-quality speakers and credible organizers.

Feasibility

A convincing and realistic organizational plan.

Submission cycle

Two rounds each year

31 December of year X−1for schools held May X through April X+1
30 June of year Xfor schools held November X through October X+1

The committee normally selects proposals within one month; the Board then decides on approval within one month after receiving the ranked recommendations.

For the current calendar, verify the exact applicable round before submission. The live IACR proposal page has historically required manual date updates.
